Tollens' test:
oxidizing an aldehyde

Scale the aldehyde and see how much silver mirror deposits. Benzaldehyde is used here as the example aldehyde — the ratio holds for any R–CHO.

Reaction scheme
Tollens' reagent (Ag⁺) needed — mmol
Silver deposited — mg
Positive test —
Conditions
  • Freshly prepared ammoniacal AgNO₃ — Tollens' reagent degrades and can become unstable if stored.
  • Clean, grease-free glass — the mirror only adheres to a clean surface.
  • Gentle warming, ~60–75°C water bath — not direct flame.
  • Ketones give no reaction — this is what distinguishes an aldehyde from a ketone.

Why 2:1: each aldehyde reduces two [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ complexes to metallic silver while it's oxidized to a carboxylate — that's the electron count, one aldehyde loses two electrons, each Ag⁺ gains one.
