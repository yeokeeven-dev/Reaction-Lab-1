Conditions
  • Anhydrous AlCl₃ — water destroys the catalyst instantly.
  • ≥1 equivalent AlCl₃, not catalytic — it complexes tightly with the ketone product, unlike Friedel–Crafts alkylation.
  • Dry solvent (e.g. DCM or CS₂), 0°C warming to room temperature.
  • No carbocation rearrangement — the acylium ion is resonance-stabilized, so the product is clean.

Why the ring doesn't over-react: the ketone product is deactivating and meta-directing, so once one acyl group is installed, the ring is far less reactive toward a second acylation — the reaction self-limits at mono-substitution.
